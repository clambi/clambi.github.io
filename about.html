Amber Abboud

My work explores experiences that resist easy categorization: states that exist before clear distinction, symbolic territories that language cannot fully separate, and forms of perception that remain fluid rather than fixed.

I create images that move between bodies, cells, faces, and patterns, treating each as a way of understanding what it feels like to exist from the inside.

Through drawing, painting, illustration, photography, collage, installation, animation, digital media, and interactive works, I investigate how meaning emerges before it settles into certainty.

I am interested in relationships between fragments, patterns, bodies, symbols, and systems.

Rather than treating systems as sterile structures of optimization or classification, I approach them as living networks of connection.

Repetition, accumulation, mapping, and arrangement become ways of exploring how parts relate to one another and how coherence emerges through those relationships.

Across my practice, art functions as a tool for orientation.

The works I create are not intended to provide definitive answers, but to offer frameworks for navigating ambiguity, transformation, and perception.

Eyes and faces often appear throughout my work as interfaces between inner and outer worlds, emphasizing perception as an active exchange between viewer, image, and environment.

In this way, art becomes a space for navigating experiences that exist before or beyond easy naming.
